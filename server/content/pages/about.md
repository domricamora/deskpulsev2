## Why it exists

Remote-work monitoring is sold almost exclusively per seat, with no ceiling. That is a
pricing model that charges you more for growing, and it lands hardest on exactly the
businesses that grow fastest: outsourcing firms and BPOs going from eight desks to eighty.

At forty seats a per-seat tool is a rounding error. At a hundred it is a line item someone
has to defend. The tools also fragment the job — one product for time, another for
screenshots, a third for payroll — so an operations manager reconciles three exports to
answer one question.

DeskPulse takes a different position on both. **One price per seat that stops at a cap**,
so headcount growth does not re-open the budget. And **one system** covering tracked time,
activity, screenshots, per-task reporting, client billing and payroll, so the numbers agree
with each other by construction.

## How we think about monitoring

Monitoring software has a deserved reputation problem. Our position is specific:

- **Consent-first, always.** Tracking runs only when the worker presses Start, with a
  persistent indicator on screen. There is no covert mode, and deploying it covertly is a
  breach of our [terms](/terms) that we will terminate an account over.
- **Proof of work, not surveillance.** The product exists to answer "what was done and
  what is it worth", not to police individuals. No keystroke content is captured. Ever.
- **The worker sees what the manager sees.** Every employee gets their own dashboard,
  their own timesheet and their own payslip from the same data their manager reads.

We think monitoring is defensible when it is transparent, proportionate and mutual — and
indefensible when it is not. The product is built to make the defensible version the easy
one.
